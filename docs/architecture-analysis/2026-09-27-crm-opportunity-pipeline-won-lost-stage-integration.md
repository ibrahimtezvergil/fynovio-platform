# CRM Opportunity Pipeline — Won/Lost as Pipeline Stages & Cross-Pipeline Move

**Status:** RESOLVED by owner 2026-09-27, first round (§3, four decisions) and second round (§6, two follow-up items, both confirmed 2026-09-27). Not yet implemented — this document is the binding design brief for a future execution plan, not a changelog. **§6.1 amends `2026-09-19-crm-phase2-authorization-delta-and-entry-stage-resolution.md`'s "OPEN DECISION RESOLUTION — Pipeline Entry Stage Semantics"** — that document's table marked "tenant has zero `PipelineDefinition`s → legal, `Open` proceeds with null version/stage" as a deliberate, resolved decision; §6.1 below reverses it. Recorded explicitly so a future reader doesn't have to reconstruct that this was a conscious reversal, not an oversight.

---

## 1. Problem statement

Today `Status` (`Draft|Open|Won|Lost`, `Opportunity.cs` L5–11) and `PipelineStageId` (nullable FK, L25) are two independent facts on `Opportunity`. Won/Lost are reached only through the dedicated `Win()`/`Lose()` aggregate methods (L124–157) and never appear as pipeline-board columns; `PipelineStage` (`PipelineStage.cs`) has no notion of a stage "kind" — every stage is implicitly an open/in-progress stage. The owner asked whether Won/Lost should instead be regular stages *inside* the pipeline (fixed `kind`, editable label, board-visible, reportable like any other stage) and, if so, what happens to open opportunities when a pipeline version changes, and whether an opportunity can move between pipelines.

This is a schema/state-machine-level change (`docs/schema/crm-sales-schema.md`'s "Domain-vs-database invariant boundary" section already exists for exactly this class of decision — cross-row/cross-table rules cannot be a single-table Postgres `CHECK`), so it goes through the project's architecture-decision workflow: real-precedent research first, then a concrete recommendation, formalized here.

## 2. Industry precedent

Three real, currently-operating, architecturally distinct products were checked (not recalled from general knowledge):

- **HubSpot** — Won/Lost *are* regular pipeline stages, structurally fixed via a probability mapping: every pipeline must include a stage mapped to 100% ("Closed won") and one mapped to 0% ("Closed lost"); forecasting/reporting requires this. Labels are documented as renameable; whether a pipeline may have more than one closed-lost-equivalent stage is not documented either way. [HubSpot default deal properties](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/hubspots-default-deal-properties). Stage-entry history is tracked via calculated properties (`Date entered current stage`, `Time in Stage [X]`) but there is no built-in "which stage do we lose deals at most" report — teams build it themselves. [HubSpot stage-calculated properties](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/stage-calculated-properties); [community thread confirming the gap](https://community.hubspot.com/t5/CRM/View-lost-deals-per-stage/m-p/377127).
- **Odoo** — a genuinely different, third shape: `crm.stage.is_won` is a boolean *flag* on a stage (Won is "whichever stage has this flag"), but **Lost is not a stage at all** — "Mark Lost" archives the record (`active=false`, probability 0) and it disappears from the pipeline. [Odoo 19 docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/sales/crm/pipeline/lost_opportunities.html). Worth citing as evidence that "both terminal states must be symmetric board columns" is a choice, not a law of nature — this project's owner deliberately asked for both Won and Lost as board-visible stages, for reporting symmetry (see §3).
- **Pipedrive** — deleting a stage that has deals in it is a manual per-deletion choice (move them to another stage, or delete them with it) — no forced mapping wizard. [Pipedrive: deleting items](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-can-i-delete-items-in-pipedrive). A deal can be moved to a *different pipeline* with the target stage picked manually (detail-view dropdown, drag-to-corner, or bulk edit) — no auto-default. [Pipedrive: moving a deal to another pipeline](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-can-i-move-a-deal-to-another-pipeline).

None of the three checked products expose anything resembling this project's `PipelineDefinitionVersion` (immutable, versioned, publishable pipeline definitions) to end users — that machinery is already this codebase's own, more rigorous, precedent for decision 2.

## 3. Decisions (owner, 2026-09-27)

### 3.1 Lost stage cardinality — single Lost stage

One system Lost stage per pipeline, not one per lost reason. Reason detail stays in the existing `LostReason` catalog (`src/Modules/CRM/Domain/LostReason.cs`) — already wired through `LoseOpportunityHandler`: if a tenant maintains active `LostReason` rows, `command.LostReason` must match one by key or name (`LoseOpportunityHandler.cs` L64–68); if the tenant has none, free text is accepted as-is. No product checked above uses one stage per loss reason; conflating reason (a business-data axis) with stage (a workflow-position axis) would also break the "exactly one Won and one Lost stage" invariant used elsewhere (see 4.2).

### 3.2 Pipeline version retirement — open opportunities stay and finish in place

**No new mechanism needed for existing opportunities' continued operation — the current code already implements this.** `PublishPipelineVersionHandler` (`CreatePipelineDraftHandler.cs` L139–228) already: finds the currently-`Published` version(s), counts opportunities still pointing at them (`impactCount`, L169–171 — this counts *every* opportunity referencing the old version, open or already closed, not just open ones; the name is a slight overstatement worth fixing if this code is touched), calls `Supersede()` on them (L172, guarded by `PipelineDefinitionVersion.cs` L101: "Only a published version can be superseded"), and publishes the new version — without touching a single `Opportunity` row. `OpenOpportunityHandler.ResolveEntryStageAsync` (L118–151) always resolves the tenant's *current* `Published` version itself — `OpenOpportunityCommand` carries no caller-supplied version id that could bypass this — so a `Superseded` version is closed to new entries automatically, exactly as decided. `impactCount` is already recorded in the outbox event and evidence record — this satisfies the "tell the operator how many opportunities are affected" instinct from the original proposal without needing a migration wizard. The rejected alternative (forced bulk stage-mapping wizard) is simply not built.

**What §5 below does add, though:** this "no new mechanism" claim covers the *existing* Status/PipelineStageId model only. Once Won/Lost become real stages (§5.1), every version published *before* that change ships has no Won/Lost-kind stages to resolve — see the one-time backfill in §5.6.

### 3.3 Cross-pipeline move — allowed, manual target stage

An open opportunity may move to a different `PipelineDefinition` (not just a different version of the same one), with the destination stage picked manually — no automatic same-name/same-order default. This matches Pipedrive's model directly (3.2 above). No command for this exists today; see 5.4.

### 3.4 Stage mandatory once Open

Once an opportunity transitions to `Open`, `PipelineStageId` becomes required. Today `Opportunity.Open(...)` accepts `pipelineStageId` as nullable (`Opportunity.cs` L102, L109–110 only requires *if* a stage is given, its version must also be given — the reverse is not required), so stage-less `Open` rows can exist. This blocks deriving `Status` display/board placement purely from `PipelineStage.Kind` (an `Open` opportunity with no stage has no `Kind` to read) and needs a backfill before the rule can be enforced (see 5.5).

## 4. Corrections to the original proposal, verified against this codebase

The initial design discussion (conducted with general CRM knowledge, not this repo) contained claims that don't hold here — recorded so a future implementer doesn't rebuild them the wrong way:

### 4.1 "A database CHECK constraint keeps `status` and `stage.kind` in sync" — wrong as stated

`stage.kind` lives on `pipeline_stages`, `status` lives on `opportunities` — a single-table Postgres `CHECK` cannot read across tables, which is precisely why `crm-sales-schema.md`'s "Domain-vs-database invariant boundary" section exists (cited above). **`Status` stays a real, aggregate-enforced column** — it is not derived at read time from `PipelineStage.Kind`. `PipelineStage.Kind` exists for board rendering and for the two enforcement points below (4.2, 4.3), not as `Status`'s source of truth. (3.4's own rule — "non-null stage required once Open" — *is* legitimately a single-table constraint, since both columns are on `opportunities`: `CHECK (status <> 'open' OR pipeline_stage_id IS NOT NULL)`, using the lower-case string values `OpportunityConfiguration.ToDb`/`FromDb` already persist (`OpportunityConfiguration.cs` L101–115), not the C# enum names. That one is fine as a real constraint; the cross-table one is not.)

**The `Draft → Lost` path needs its own answer, not left implicit.** `Opportunity.Lose()` only rejects an already-`Won`/`Lost` opportunity (L148) — `Draft → Lost` is legal today and is real, tested behavior (`OpportunityStateMachineTests.cs`, multiple cases call `.Lose(...)` on a freshly-created Draft opportunity, never opened). A Draft opportunity can have a null `PipelineDefinitionVersionId`/`PipelineStageId`, so there is nothing for `Lose()` to resolve a Lost-kind stage from in that path. Resolution, consistent with the rest of this section: **`Draft → Lost` leaves `PipelineDefinitionVersionId`/`PipelineStageId` null** — an opportunity lost before ever being opened never entered a pipeline, so it has no board position, and the 3.4/4.1 "stage required" `CHECK` only fires on `status = 'open'`, so this is not a gap in that constraint. `Win()` has no equivalent path (L127 requires `Status == Open`), so it always has a version/stage to resolve from.

### 4.2 Authorization boundary — the board must not let a drag-and-drop become a silent `Win`

`WinOpportunityHandler`, `LoseOpportunityHandler`, and `ChangePipelineStageHandler` already authorize against three distinct Access actions — `crm.opportunity.win`, `crm.opportunity.lose`, `crm.opportunity.change_stage` (each file's `ActionKeyValue` constant). This separation **must survive** Won/Lost becoming stages: dragging a card onto the Won/Lost column must keep dispatching `WinOpportunityCommand`/`LoseOpportunityCommand`, never `ChangePipelineStageCommand`. Concretely, `ChangePipelineStageHandler` gains one more rejection alongside its existing checks (L67–84: stage must exist, belong to the opportunity's version, be active, and — if `EnforceAllowedTransitions` is on — be a configured edge): **reject any `targetStage.Kind != Open`** unconditionally, regardless of the tenant's `EnforceAllowedTransitions` setting (defense in depth — that flag is tenant-configurable and must not be able to turn off this rule).

### 4.3 "Pipeline versions are immutable" — true for the published stage *set*, not for individual stages

`PipelineStage` already has `Deactivate()`/`Activate()`/`Archive()` (L42–56), and `ce3710a` (already on `main`) changed how inactive stages render on the board. What's actually immutable is the *set of stages a published version was validated with* (`PublishPipelineVersionHandler.Validate`, L219–227) — a stage's `IsActive`/`IsArchived` lifecycle can still change post-publish. Won/Lost system stages need their own, narrower rule (4.4), not a claim that nothing about a published version's stages ever changes.

### 4.4 Won/Lost system stages are locked, but only partially — scope the lock explicitly

"Fixed kind, renameable label, can't be deleted, always sorts last" is a narrow, hardcoded special case for exactly two stages per version — **it is not the general per-field/per-stage locking mechanism** that `docs/architecture-analysis/15_TENANT_PROCESS_CUSTOMIZATION.md` §6 (research repo) leaves unresolved (`OwnerScope: Tenant | Network`, per-field/per-step locking has no schema or code yet). Don't let this design quietly become the first implementation of that open item — it's a special case, not a precedent to generalize from.

## 5. Concrete shape (for the execution plan, not built here)

1. **`PipelineStage.Kind`** — new enum `Open` (default) | `Won` | `Lost`. Every published version must contain exactly one `Won`-kind and one `Lost`-kind stage, both `IsActive`, neither the entry stage, both sorted after every `Open`-kind stage — extend `PublishPipelineVersionHandler.Validate` (L219–227) with these checks, and reject any `PipelineStageTransition` edge that targets a `Won`/`Lost`-kind stage (dead config — `Win()`/`Lose()` bypass the transition graph entirely, per 4.2/item 3 below; `ChangePipelineStage` can never reach them). `Won`/`Lost`-kind stages are system-created when a draft is created (not user-addable/removable in `CreatePipelineDraftHandler`'s stage list), only `Name` (label) and `SortOrder`-within-the-trailing-pair are editable.
2. **`Opportunity.ClosedFromStageId`** (nullable `long`) — set once, inside `Win()`/`Lose()`, to whatever `PipelineStageId` held *before* the method overwrites it. Needed for "which stage do we lose deals from most" reporting (HubSpot's own documented gap, §2 — this project can do better).
3. **`Win()`/`Lose()` must resolve and set the tenant's Won/Lost stage.** Today they only flip `Status` (L124–157) and are pure aggregate methods with no database access — resolving "which stage has `Kind == Won`/`Lost` for this version" is a query, so `WinOpportunityHandler`/`LoseOpportunityHandler` must do that lookup and pass the resolved stage id into `Win(wonStageId)`/`Lose(lostReason, lostStageId)` (signature change on both). The aggregate then sets `ClosedFromStageId = PipelineStageId` then `PipelineStageId = <passed-in stage>` before flipping `Status` — otherwise "Won/Lost are stages" is cosmetic and the board can't place closed opportunities correctly. Exception: `Draft → Lost` (4.1) has no version to resolve from and leaves both fields null, same as today.
4. **`OpportunityStageHistory`** table (`OpportunityId`, `PipelineStageId`, `EnteredAt`, `ExitedAt` nullable) — one row opened on every stage entry (`Open()`, `ChangeStage()`, `Win()`, `Lose()`), closed on the next transition. Feeds funnel/time-in-stage reporting; `ClosedFromStageId` alone is not enough for full funnel analysis.
5. **New `MoveOpportunityToPipeline` command** (decision 3.3) — validates the target stage is `Kind == Open` and belongs to a `Published` version of the target `PipelineDefinition`; actor supplies the target stage explicitly, no default. Separate Access action, same authorization pattern as the three existing opportunity commands (4.2).
6. **One-time backfill, in this order, before any `CHECK` from 4.1 is added:**
   a. Create a `Won`-kind and a `Lost`-kind stage on **every existing `Published` and `Superseded` `PipelineDefinitionVersion`** (`Draft`/`Archived` versions never had opportunities open against them — skip). Without this, item 3 has nothing to resolve for any opportunity closed before this change ships, and every `Win`/`Lose` on current data breaks. This is a deliberate, one-time exception to 4.3's "published stage set is immutable" — state it as such, not silently.
   b. For every already-`Won`/`Lost` opportunity whose `PipelineDefinitionVersionId` is not null: set `ClosedFromStageId` to its *current* `PipelineStageId` (that already holds the last open stage it was in — `Win`/`Lose` never touch it today), then overwrite `PipelineStageId` with the new Won/Lost-kind stage from step (a) for its version. Without this they'd keep rendering in an open column on the redesigned board.
   c. For every `Open`, stage-less opportunity (decision 3.4): assign it a stage via a deterministic fallback, not a manual per-row review (a migration can't pause for human input). **Prefer the opportunity's own `PipelineDefinitionVersionId` when it is already set** (its version's `IsEntry` stage) — only fall back to `CrmSettings.DefaultPipelineDefinitionId` → that pipeline's current `Published` version → its `IsEntry` stage when the opportunity's own version is also null. Backfilling from the tenant default unconditionally would silently move some opportunities to a different pipeline than the one they were actually opened against, contradicting 3.2/3.3 (a pipeline change should be an explicit, visible move, never a side effect of a backfill). If a tenant has no resolvable default at all, flag those rows for the owner rather than guessing. **Confirmed by the owner, with a required run-order — see §6.2.**
7. **No CRM-enabled tenant may ever be pipeline-less again — resolved as belt-and-suspenders, not either/or (§6.1):**
   a. **Auto-provision.** When the CRM module is enabled for a tenant, a minimal default pipeline is created and published right after — one `Open`-kind entry stage plus the mandatory `Won`/`Lost`-kind stages from item 1 above. This is orchestrated at the **Host** level (an `enable-tenant-module`-flow step, or a module-enabled event CRM consumes), calling the existing, already-idempotent `ProvisionPipelineHandler` (`AlreadyProvisioned` status if retried) — **not** added to `EnableTenantModuleHandler` itself, which lives in `src/Modules/Access/` and must not gain a dependency on CRM application code. This mirrors the shape `DevSeeder` already uses: enable the module (L140), then a separate call to `CrmDevSeed.EnsurePipelineAsync` (L148) — production gets the same two-step shape, not a shortcut dev doesn't also take.
   b. **Domain guard, not just a happy-path default.** Auto-provisioning alone doesn't make the pipeline-less state unreachable: enabling the module (Access's `DbContext`) and provisioning the pipeline (CRM's `DbContext`) are separate transactions, so a failure between them leaves a tenant CRM-enabled with no pipeline until a retry. `OpenOpportunityHandler.ResolveEntryStageAsync`'s two `(null, null)` returns (no `PipelineDefinition` at all, L132–133; a `PipelineDefinition` exists but has no `Published` version, L140–141) both become a clear domain error instead — `Opportunity.Open(...)`'s `pipelineStageId`/`pipelineDefinitionVersionId` parameters stop being nullable on the happy path once this ships. `ProvisionPipelineHandler`'s idempotency makes retrying the auto-provision step safe.
   c. **Close the reopen-the-gap path.** `SetPipelineLifecycleHandler` (L40–44) only blocks deactivating/archiving a pipeline that is a tenant's *configured* `CrmSettings.DefaultPipelineDefinitionId` — a tenant with no `CrmSettings` row at all can archive its only active pipeline today and land right back in the pipeline-less state item (a)/(b) just closed. Add a "this is the tenant's only active, non-archived pipeline" guard alongside the existing default-pipeline one.
   d. **One-time provisioning pass for tenants that reached pipeline-less state before this ships.** Low-risk here specifically because there are no production tenants yet (confirmed local-dev-only) — this can run directly rather than needing a staged rollout, but still goes through the same idempotent `ProvisionPipelineHandler` path as (a), not a one-off script.

## 6. Amendments and confirmations (owner, 2026-09-27, second round — RESOLVED)

### 6.1 No CRM-enabled tenant may stay pipeline-less — amends 2026-09-19

**Resolved: both (a) and (b), not either/or** — see §5 item 7 for the four-part shape (auto-provision on module enablement, orchestrated at Host level, never inside Access's `EnableTenantModuleHandler`; a real domain-error guard in `OpenOpportunityHandler`, since auto-provisioning alone doesn't make the state unreachable across two separate transactions; closing `SetPipelineLifecycleHandler`'s reopen-the-gap path; a one-time provisioning pass for any tenant already in this state). Justification for reversing 2026-09-19's "legal" table entry: no production tenant exists yet (this project is local-dev-only, confirmed separately), so the cost of tightening the invariant now is zero, and a CRM-enabled tenant that can't open an opportunity or show a board because it has no pipeline at all was never a useful state to preserve — it was tolerated in 2026-09-19 as a byproduct of not having built auto-provisioning yet, not as a deliberately desired end state.

### 6.2 §5 item 6.c backfill fallback — confirmed, with a required ordering

Confirmed acceptable as written (prefer the opportunity's own pipeline version's entry stage; fall back to the tenant's default pipeline only when the opportunity's own version is also null). **Sequencing requirement added:** run §5 item 7's provisioning fixes (6.1) *before* the §5 item 6 backfill, and the backfill before 4.1's `CHECK` is added. `ResolveEntryStageAsync` always sets `PipelineDefinitionVersionId`/`PipelineStageId` together or leaves both null (never one without the other), so under this ordering the "own version" branch of the fallback is expected to cover close to zero real rows, and the "flag unresolvable rows for the owner" escape hatch should end up empty — not a live decision point at backfill time, a safety net for a case this ordering is designed to eliminate. Execute as an idempotent Host operator command (same shape as `provision-crm-pipeline`), each backfilled row writing an `EvidenceRecord` under a system/operator `PrincipalRef` (matching `CrmDevSeed`'s seed-operator pattern) — not a raw EF migration silently mutating rows with no audit trail.

## 7. Explicitly deferred, not decided here

- The general per-field/per-stage tenant-customization locking mechanism (4.4) — stays with `15_TENANT_PROCESS_CUSTOMIZATION.md` §6 in the research repo, unresolved.
- Whether `PipelineStage.Kind` gets denormalized onto `opportunities` for query/report performance. Default: no — derive via join when reporting; revisit only if a real performance problem shows up.
- Whether a pipeline may have more than one `Won`-kind stage (e.g., "Won — upsell pending"). Not requested, not precedented by any product checked; out of scope unless raised again.
- A stable, version-independent stage key for cross-version reporting (so v1's "Teklif" and v2's "Teklif" aggregate on the same funnel row instead of being treated as unrelated stages). Raised in the original discussion, not decided here — `PipelineStage` has no such key today (`Name` is only unique within a version). Needed before serious funnel/stage-history reporting across pipeline versions is built on top of item 5.4 above.

## 8. Plain-language summary

Bugün bir fırsatın "durumu" (Draft/Open/Won/Lost) ile "hangi aşamada olduğu" birbirinden bağımsız iki bilgi. Bu tasarımla Won ve Lost, panoda görünen sıradan birer aşama haline geliyor (adı değiştirilebilir, ama silinemez/taşınamaz sistem aşamaları) — ama arkadaki "kazanma/kaybetme" mantığı (satır kontrolü, kayıp nedeni zorunluluğu, tutar yuvarlama) aynı iki komuta (`Win`/`Lose`) bağlı kalıyor; panoda kartı bu sütunlara sürüklemek sadece bu komutları tetikliyor, aşama değiştirme yetkisi hiçbir zaman "kazandırma/kaybettirme" yetkisine dönüşmüyor. Pipeline (satış akışı) düzenlendiğinde açık fırsatlar zaten — kod zaten böyle çalışıyor, yeni bir şey yapmaya gerek yok — eski sürümde kalıp orada kapanıyor; sadece yeni fırsat girişi eski sürüme kapatılıyor. Bir fırsatı tamamen başka bir satış akışına taşımak mümkün olacak, hedef aşamayı kullanıcı elle seçecek. Tek yeni zorunluluk: bir fırsat "Open" olduğunda artık mutlaka bir aşamaya atanmış olması gerekecek (bugün atanmadan da açılabiliyor). Bunun için, ve geçmişte kapanmış (Won/Lost) fırsatların yeni panoda doğru sütunda görünmesi için, mevcut verilerde tek seferlik bir düzeltme (backfill) gerekiyor — hem eski pipeline sürümlerine Won/Lost aşaması eklenecek hem de o sürümlere bağlı geçmiş fırsatlar bu yeni aşamalara bağlanacak. Hiç açılmadan doğrudan Draft'tan kaybedilen fırsatlar (bu, sistemde zaten olan ve kullanılan bir yol) bir pipeline'a hiç girmediği için aşamasız kalmaya devam edecek — bu bir eksiklik değil, tutarlı bir kural.

**İkinci tur kararlar (2026-09-27):** Bugüne kadar bir tenant CRM'i açıp hiç pipeline kurmadan da fırsat açabiliyordu (2026-09-19'da bilerek öyle bırakılmıştı). Artık bunu tersine çeviriyoruz: CRM açıldığında otomatik olarak minimal bir pipeline kurulacak (Açık + Won + Lost), ayrıca hiç pipeline'ı olmayan bir tenant'ta fırsat açmayı engelleyen gerçek bir hata mesajı eklenecek, ve bir tenant'ın tek pipeline'ını arşivleyip yeniden pipeline'sız kalabildiği küçük bir boşluk kapatılacak. Şu an prod'da tenant olmadığı için bunun maliyeti yok. Backfill (geçmiş verideki düzeltme) mantığı onaylandı, ama sırası önemli: önce bu pipeline-kurma düzeltmeleri, sonra backfill, en son da veritabanı kısıtlaması (CHECK) eklenecek — bu sırayla "çözülemeyen satır" diye owner'a kalacak bir durum pratikte kalmıyor.
